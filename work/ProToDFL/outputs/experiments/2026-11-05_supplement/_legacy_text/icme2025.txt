Abstract—Decentralized
Federated
Learning
(DFL)
is
a
privacy-preserving framework that eliminates the need for a
central server, enabling direct communication between clients
to save communication resources. However, existing approaches
do not adequately address the high data heterogeneity among
communication neighbors and often employ inefficient neighbor
selection strategies. We propose ProToDFL, a novel framework
that leverages prototype learning for dynamic topology optimiza-
tion in DFL. ProToDFL constructs communication topologies
through selecting 1-hop and 2-hop neighbors based on the
Kullback-Leibler divergence between prototype vectors, ensuring
alignment with local data distributions. To prevent topology
splitting during optimization, it develops a cut-edge detection
mechanism. Additionally, ProToDFL incorporates a Stop-and-
Wait strategy inspired by exponential back-off to minimize pro-
totype vector transmissions. We provide theoretical convergence
analysis of ProToDFL within a general non-convex framework
for decentralized training. Experimental results on multiple real-
world datasets show that ProToDFL significantly outperforms
existing DFL methods, particularly in heterogeneous and sparse
environments.

I. INTRODUCTION
Federated Learning (FL) enables distributed model training
while keeping data local, reducing privacy risks in sensitive
fields like healthcare and finance [1]. However, traditional
Centralized Federated Learning (CFL) faces challenges such
as communication bottlenecks and single points of failure.
This has driven the shift to Decentralized Federated Learning
(DFL), which replaces the central server with peer-to-peer
(P2P) communication. In DFL, clients directly exchange and
update models with their neighbors, enhancing efficiency,

A key challenge in FL is data heterogeneity, where clients
hold local datasets with differing distributions. This variation
can degrade performance during model aggregation, as param-
eters trained on distinct data may conflict. To address this,
methods like regularization [4], [5], knowledge distillation
[6], and hyper networks [7] adapt models to each client’s
data. While mitigate the effects of data heterogeneity, these
approaches are primarily designed for centralized federated
learning, relying on a central server for coordination.
Constructing an optimal global federated topology is crucial
for real-world applications, such as enabling hospitals with
similar data distributions to collaborate and enhance disease
prediction and diagnosis accuracy [8]. In DFL, the absence of
a central server requires peer-to-peer communication among
limited neighbors. This decentralized setup necessitates select-
ing similar collaborators in global topology to align clients’
data characteristics, ensuring efficient learning. Well-matched
neighbors improve performance, while heterogeneous ones
cause inefficiencies and inconsistent updates. Thus, construct-
ing a globally optimized communication topology that con-
nects compatible clients remains a key challenge in DFL [9].
Traditional DFL methods [2], [10]–[12] often use personal-
ized approaches to mitigate data heterogeneity among neigh-
bors, leading to poor performance in highly heterogeneous
topologies. To address this, dynamic neighbor selection meth-
ods like Jiang et al. [13] and Chen et al. [14] adjust connections
based on neighbors’ local model performance, improving ac-
curacy. Additionally, graph-based techniques such as collabo-
ration graph networks [15], [16] and pFedGraph [17] optimize
network topology for diverse data environments. However,
these methods struggle to accurately measure data distribution
heterogeneity in the global topology, causing inefficiencies and
higher communication costs. Thus, achieving fast and efficient
global topology optimization in fully decentralized settings
remains a significant challenge.

that leverages prototype learning for dynamic global topology

• We present a framework called ProToDFL that integrates
prototype learning into dynamic topology optimization,

III. PROTODFL FRAMEWORK
A. Framework Overview
In this section, we propose the Prototype-Based Commu-
nication Topology Optimization of Decentralized Federated
Learning (ProToDFL) framework to address the dual opti-
mization problem Eq. (2) in decentralized setting. The main
idea of our approach is to select neighbors and optimize the
global topology network based on the KL divergence between
prototype vectors.
The overall ProToDFL framework consists of four main
stages: (1) Prototype Computation (2) Neighbor Divergence
Calculation (3) 2-Hop Neighbor Selection (4) Topology and
Parameter Update. The framework is shown in Figure 1.

F. Stop-and-Wait Strategy
To further reduce the communication overhead of sending
low-dimensional prototype vectors to neighbors, we propose
an improved stop-and-wait method inspired by exponential
backoff. Nodes with fewer neighbors or lower bandwidth
can quickly identify optimal communication partners. If a
node’s current neighbors are not ideal in global communication
round t, it can connect to a more appropriate optimal neigh-
bor through a 2-hop connection. If no 2-hop neighbors are
found for an extended period, the node is considered to have
achieved optimal topology and stops sending prototype vectors
for further optimization. Specifically, the client pauses one
global communication round before resending its prototype
vector, with the waiting time increasing exponentially if no
improvements are found.

IV. EXPERIMENTS
A. Experiment Setup
1) Dataset and Data partition: We evaluate our ProToDFL
framework on three datasets: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 [18],
and Tiny-Imagenet [19], using three distinct data partitioning
scenarios to simulate real-world distributions. The Dirichlet
Partition [20] partitions training data based on a Dirichlet
distribution with α = 0.3. The Pathological Partition [21]
assigns each client a limited subset of 2 classes for CIFAR-
10, 20 for CIFAR-100, and 40 for Tiny-ImageNet. The Cross-
Dataset Partition [22] combines subsets from CIFAR-10 and
CIFAR-100 using either Dirichlet or pathological partitioning
methods. To support diverse classification tasks, clients share
only the feature extractor parameters, while keeping the clas-
sifiers local.
2) FL baselines: We compare our proposed ProToDFL
framework with several baselines in both CFL and DFL.
The simplest baseline, Local, involves each client training
independently on their own data without communication. In
CFL, we include FedAvg [23], FedAvg-FT [24], and Ditto
[25]. For DFL, baselines include GossipFL [12] , D-PSGD
[26], DA-PFL [27], DisPFL [10] and DFedSAM [2]. Detailed
descriptions of these baseline methods are provided in the
supplementary materials.
3) Implementation Details and Backbone: We maintain
consistent experimental settings across all baselines and con-
duct 500 communication rounds. The number of clients is set
to 100, divided into 10 groups to simulate real-world scenarios
with similar collaborators, where data is homogeneous within
groups and heterogeneous across groups. In each round, we
train with a batch size of 128 for 5 local epochs, using SGD
as the optimizer (SAM for DFedSAM) at a learning rate of
0.1. All experiments use ResNet-18 with group normalization
replacing batch normalization for stability.
4) Graph Topology and Communication Setup: We gener-
ate the initial topology as a static, undirected graph, where
each client is randomly connected to 10 neighbors, ensuring a
connected network. Results for experiments with dynamic
topology are provided in the supplementary materials.

B. Performance Evaluation
1) Baselines Comparison: As shown in Table II, ProToDFL
achieves consistent improvements over a variety of strong
baselines, including recent sota methods like DisPFL and DA-
PFL. For example, on CIFAR-100 (Dirichlet) and (Patho-
logical), ProToDFL surpasses DisPFL by about 1.70% and
3.53%, respectively, and outperforms DA-PFL by approxi-
mately 4.51% and 2.86%. On Tiny-ImageNet, ProToDFL leads
DA-PFL by roughly 4.43% (Dirichlet) and 5.01% (Patholog-
ical), and on the challenging CIFAR-10 + CIFAR-100 com-
bined scenario, it provides a clear margin over all baselines.
These results highlight the strong benefits of our prototype-
based global topology construction, enabling efficient col-
laboration among nodes with similar data distributions and
delivering superior performance in complex, heterogeneous
settings. A detailed analysis of the convergence speed for
all baseline experiments is provided in the supplementary
materials.

confirm that our ProToDFL framework effectively addresses
the challenges posed through varying network connectivity and
sparse communication, thereby enhancing both the scalability
and adaptability of decentralized federated learning.

performance and efficiency in sparse network settings.
V. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose ProToDFL, a decentralized fed-
erated learning framework that optimizes global topology
through dynamic neighbor selection and prototype learning.
Theoretical analysis shows that minimizing KL divergence en-
hances convergence by reducing gradient discrepancies among
clients. Experiments confirm ProToDFL’s superior perfor-
mance in heterogeneous environments, significantly improving
model accuracy and ensuring robust communication network
connectivity. These results demonstrate ProToDFL’s effective-
ness for adaptive distributed machine learning. Future work
will focus on more efficient communication strategies to boost
performance and broaden ProToDFL’s applicability.
